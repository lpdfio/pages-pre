Chart

A chart drawn from numbers, written like a table: series are its columns, each category a row and each value a cell, a value for each series in the series’ order. A chart with one series needs no series. It takes its box in the flow as an image does, and the engine draws its axis, its steps, its labels and its legend by fixed rules, so a chart has few attributes and no styling language. It draws bars, lines, areas, pies and donuts, and sparklines.

  • XML
  • JavaScript
  • Python
  • Java
  • C#
  • PHP

The engine draws a value axis on the left, from zero in round steps, $0, $10,000 up to $50,000, with a light gridline at each, a bar for each category with its label under it, and no legend, since there is one series. There is no title attribute: a text above the chart is its title, and paginate="no" on the column keeps the two on one page.

Attributes

Section titled “Attributes”
Attribute Type Default Description
type string — Required. The kind of chart: bar, line, area, pie, donut or sparkline
height token/length — Required. Height of the whole chart: a length, or a name from the height row of tokens. The axis, the labels and the legend take room inside it and the width, and the plot gets the rest
width token/length fill Width of the whole chart
direction string vertical Bars only: vertical, or horizontal, which lists the categories down the left side. See Horizontal bars
stacked boolean false Bars and areas only: stacks the bars of a category, or the areas of the series. See Stacked bars and Areas
prefix, suffix string — Written around every number of the chart: $, %, ms. See Numbers
decimals 0–6 as written The decimals of the value labels, and of the shares in the legend of a pie or a donut, which are whole numbers unless it says otherwise
min, max number — A number the value axis reaches at least. They only widen it. See The axis and the labels
thousands-mark string , One character between the groups of three digits, or empty for none
decimal-mark string . One character before the decimals
gap token/length s The space between the plot and its step labels, its category labels and its legend
bar-gap token/length a tenth of a bar The space between the bars of one category. Bars only
category-gap token/length a quarter of a category The space between categories. Bars only
legend string bottom with two series, none with one, right for a pie or a donut bottom, top, right or none
value-labels boolean false Writes each value at the end of its bar or over its point, or under it at a low point
stroke-width length 1.5pt, 1pt for a sparkline The width of every line of a line, an area or a sparkline. 0 draws none, so an area is its fill alone
hole 0.1 to 0.9 0.6 Donuts only: the hole as a share of the donut’s size. See Pies and donuts
hole-text string — Donuts only: a text in the middle of the hole, as it should read. data-hole-text reads it from the data
font, font-size, color the text’s around it The chart’s text: its labels, steps and legend, and the color of its zero line. A sparkline, which has no text, draws its line in color
alt string — What the chart shows, for a tagged document. See Accessibility

A series takes label, its entry in the legend, and color, its bars’, line’s or area’s. A category takes label, written by its bars or under its point, and color, the color of its bar in a bar chart of one series, or of its slice in a pie or a donut. A value is a plain number, such as 41200, -3.5 or 0.25, and an empty <value/> is no value: no bar, and a gap in a line. All three take data-source, data-if and data-if-not, series and category data-label and data-color, and value data-value. See From the data.

direction is for bars, and stacked for bars and areas: on another kind each is an error that names it, such as direction is for a bar chart, and this is a line chart. hole and hole-text are for donuts, and an error on any other kind.

Stacked bars

Section titled “Stacked bars”

With stacked="true", the bars of a category stand on each other in the order of the series, and the axis runs to the largest total. With value-labels, the total of each stack is written at its end. A stacked value can’t be negative.

Lines

Section titled “Lines”

type="line" draws a line through the values of each series, a point at the middle of each category. Visits per week, this year against last:

  • XML
  • JavaScript
  • Python
  • Java
  • C#
  • PHP
  • The axis fits the values: it runs from a round step under the lowest to one over the highest. A point is read by its height, and on an axis from zero a line far from it comes out flat. The axis doesn’t cross zero where the values don’t, so a count never shows below it, and min="0" brings it down to zero.
  • A missing value breaks the line: an empty <value/>, or null in the data, ends the line at the point before, and it starts again at the point after, so the reader sees the gap. A value with no value next to it, on either side, is a dot.
  • The labels under the plot are every one where they all fit on one line, and else every 2nd, 3rd or 5th, starting with the first: the smallest step at which they fit. Every point keeps its place, so thirty days in 300pt write Nov 1, Nov 6, Nov 11 and on. A label still too wide on its own wraps and shortens, as on bars.
  • stroke-width sets the width of every line, 1.5pt by default. Every line takes the same width: which line is the current one and which the comparison is told by the series’ color.
  • value-labels writes each value over its point, or under it where the point is lower than the points beside it, the first and the last included, since its line doesn’t run there. A label that would cover another series’ label there stays over its point.

Areas

Section titled “Areas”

type="area" draws the line of each series and fills it down to zero, faded so that the gridlines, and an area behind it, show through. Its axis keeps zero, as an axis of bars does. With stacked="true", each series is filled down to the one under it, and the axis runs to the largest total: the parts of a whole over time.

  • XML
  • JavaScript
  • Python
  • Java
  • C#
  • PHP
  • A missing value breaks an area that isn’t stacked, as it breaks a line. In a stacked area it counts as zero, so the series above still sit on something: in March, the backups sit on the images.
  • A stacked value can’t be negative, and with value-labels a stacked area writes the total of each category over its top.
  • The labels under the plot skip as a line’s do.
  • stroke-width="0" draws no line: the area is its fill alone.

Pies and donuts

Section titled “Pies and donuts”

type="pie" draws a slice for each category, clockwise from the top, as wide as its share of the whole, in the largest circle the plot holds. It has no axis and takes no series. type="donut" is a pie with a hole. Where the money went, with the color of each slice from the data:

With no data, it renders as written: one slice for Rent.

  • The legend is at the right, and lists each slice’s color, its label and its share of the whole as a percent: Rent 22%, Salaries 73%, Travel 5%. legend places it at the bottom or the top, and none drops it, for a chart that something else names. The share is a whole number unless decimals gives it decimals, and it is written with decimal-mark. prefix and suffix belong to values, so a share has neither. A label too wide for the legend shortens, and its share stays.
  • The colors are the palette’s, in the order of the categories, started over past the eighth. color, or data-color from the data, sets the color of a slice.
  • A zero or a missing value draws no slice. A zero still has its entry in the legend, 0%, and a missing value, an empty <value/>, has its label alone.
  • hole sets a donut’s hole as a share of its size, from 0.1 to 0.9, 0.6 by default: 0.8 is a thin ring and 0.5 a thick one.
  • hole-text, or data-hole-text from the data, is written in the middle of the hole as it is given, such as a total or a share. The engine adds nothing up. A text too wide for the hole wraps at spaces onto three lines at most, and what still doesn’t fit ends in an ellipsis. A hole too small for a line of text holds none.
  • value-labels, min, max and stroke-width change nothing on a pie or a donut: it has no axis, writes no value beside a slice and draws no line. They are still read, so a min that isn’t a number is refused.
  • Refused, with an error that names it: a negative value, values that add up to zero, a series, direction or stacked, and hole or hole-text on any kind but a donut.

Sparklines

Section titled “Sparklines”

type="sparkline" draws one small line through its whole box, with no axis, no labels and no legend, for a table cell: the first value at the left edge and the last at the right, the lowest at the bottom and the highest at the top. The trend of the last weeks, for each product:

weeks is read from each product, as paths inside a data-source are.

  • One series. A sparkline takes no series: one is an error that names it, a sparkline has one series, and takes no <series>.
  • Its color is the chart’s own color, since a sparkline has no text: color="text-muted". Without it, the line is the palette’s first color, whatever the color of the text around it. stroke-width sets its width, 1pt by default.
  • min and max widen it as they widen an axis: the same min="0" max="150" on each sparkline of a table draws them all on one scale.
  • A missing value breaks it, as it breaks a line.

From the data

Section titled “From the data”

data-source repeats a category for each item of a list, as it repeats any element, and data-label sets its label from the item. With no data, the chart renders as written: one bar for Jan.

The number of series can come from the data too: data-source on series repeats a series for each item, and on value repeats a category’s values from a list, in the same order. One template then compares two builds or three:

  • A value from the data is a number, or a string that is a plain number. null and a missing key are no value: no bar, and a gap in a line. Anything else is an error that names the path, such as <value data-value="amount">: '41,200' is not a plain number.
  • data-label takes a string or a number, so a year or a week number of the data is a label as it is.
  • data-color sets the color of a series or a category: a script that writes the data can mark a bar red.
  • A category holds a value for each series, after the data is bound. One with more or fewer is an error that names it: category 'Q1' has 1 value, and the chart has 2 series. The order of the values is the data’s to keep.
  • data-if and data-if-not on an element with data-source are read once, before the list, as everywhere. See Combining attributes.

The axis and the labels

Section titled “The axis and the labels”
  • The value axis of bars and areas starts at zero, and a line’s fits its values (see Lines). It runs in about five round steps, each 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, with a light gridline at each. Fewer steps are drawn where their labels would crowd.
  • A negative value takes the axis below zero, and a zero line is drawn in the chart’s color.
  • min and max only widen the axis: max="100" makes it reach 100 at least, for percentages, or for charts meant to be compared side by side, and a value past them still fits. No bar is cut off, and none starts above zero. On a line, min="0" brings the axis down to zero.
  • A category label wider than its room wraps at spaces onto three lines at most, and what still doesn’t fit ends in an ellipsis, so a chart always renders.
  • The legend lists each series with its label and its color: under the chart with two series or more, and none with one, unless legend places it. A pie or a donut lists each slice instead, at the right (see Pies and donuts). A sparkline has none. Under or above the chart it takes half its height at most, and at the right its height and a third of its width: the entries that don’t fit give way to an ellipsis.
  • In a horizontal chart, a category thinner than a line of text has no label, so the labels don’t run into each other.

Numbers

Section titled “Numbers”

The steps of the axis and the value labels are numbers the engine writes. The marks are thousands-mark and decimal-mark, with prefix and suffix around the number, and any style is two characters: thousands-mark="." decimal-mark="," writes 1.234,5, and a space for thousands-mark writes 1 234,5. The two marks the same are an error.

  • XML
  • JavaScript
  • Python
  • Java
  • C#
  • PHP
  • The steps are written in full, $100,000, never short as $100k. A short axis comes from the data: values in thousands and suffix="k" write $412k on the steps and the value labels alike, in any language’s abbreviation.
  • A value label has the decimals of decimals, else the decimals the value was written with: 4.10 writes 4.10. It rounds half up, so 72.25 with one decimal is 72.3. The steps take the decimals their size needs.

Colors and spacing

Section titled “Colors and spacing”

Without color, the series, or the categories of a pie or a donut, take a fixed palette of eight in order, and it starts over past the eighth. color takes a color or a name from the document’s colors, on a series, or on a category in a bar chart of one series, a pie or a donut, as data-color does from the data.

gap, bar-gap and category-gap set the room, and legend picks one of fixed places:

In code

Section titled “In code”

The builders have a chart that holds LChart.series and LChart.category, and each category holds an LChart.value, in the language’s case. ChartType names the kinds: ChartType.Bar, ChartType.Line, ChartType.Area, ChartType.Pie, ChartType.Donut and ChartType.Sparkline. A builder has no data binding, so the program turns its own list into categories. LChart.value takes one number, written in full with no culture’s marks and no exponent, or a string that is a number, such as '0.250', whose decimals a value label keeps. No number is no value.

Pages

Section titled “Pages”

A chart never splits across pages. When it doesn’t fit, it moves whole to the next page, and one taller than a page is cut at its bottom. Its title is a text of its own, which could stay behind at the foot of a page: paginate="no" on a column that holds both keeps them together, as in the first example.

Accessibility

Section titled “Accessibility”

In a tagged document, with pdfua or a pdfa of level a, a chart is one figure with its alt, which says what the chart shows: alt="Revenue per month, highest in March". alt="" marks it as decoration. A PDF/UA document needs it on each chart outside a header or a footer, and refuses a chart without it.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”

A chart holds 10,000 categories and 100 series at most, counted after the data is bound. More is an error that names the limit and the list: a chart holds 10,000 categories at most; the data of 'fixtures' gives 50,000. A chart at both limits renders inside the render’s time limit.

A value, the total of a stack, min and max are 1,000,000,000,000,000 at most either side of zero, where every whole number is still exact. Past it is an error that names the category: category 'Q1' has the value 1.7e308, and a chart holds values of 1,000,000,000,000,000 at most, either side of zero.
